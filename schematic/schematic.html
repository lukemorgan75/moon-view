On the synopsis

Smyrna and Philadelphia

The chart’s papers

Candlesticks are churches, and so are the two olive trees. They are the two letters he did not condemn, left to light the second temple. They stand against the whore and the two-horned beast. He also identifies them with the 144,000 until a killing he had not seen. They have power at the trumpets before they prophesy in sackcloth.

An earlier draft read them as the two Testaments, their death as the scriptures neglected, and their rising as the gospel preached to every nation. He then said that preaching had not happened. The chart’s own papers use the two churches.

On the synopsis
A draft on the Goths

Visigoths and Ostrogoths

Both drafts

The two candlesticks are the church of the Visigoths and the church of the Ostrogoths, both descended from the churches of Asia and Greece. The fire from their mouth is war. The testimony is the churches that take part in those wars.

They were witnesses of the religion which they received from the Churches of Asia and Greece.
Very remarkable witnesses against the Church of Rome, because both of them conquered Italy and reigned over that city.
In the wars of the four first trumpets fire proceeded out of their mouth and devoured their enemies, and they had power to turn the waters into blood and to smite the earth with all plagues as often as they would: which is the proper character of the two witnesses.

These two might not be all the churches of God in the western empire, but for their eminence might be chosen to represent them all. Smyrna and Philadelphia are the two primitive lamps he does not condemn, and he derives these later churches from them.

Apart from the wars, he counts as witnessing their refusal of the mark and their keeping the testimony of Jesus. The sealed company get the victory over the beast and over his image. Singing with harps is prophesying. “In their mouth was found no lie.” They supply the lamps with oil by maintaining teachers, and by that prophesying they torment those who dwell on the earth.

In this draft the doctrine he says they witnessed is that the Father is greater than the Son. He quotes Theodoret: the Goths say that, and will not call the Son a creature, and he says they were falsely called Arians for it.

The woe trumpets

Sackcloth

Unfinished when he wrote

The 42 months, the woman’s stay in the wilderness, the treading of the holy city, and the witnesses in sackcloth are one period. It runs from the woe trumpets to the killing of the witnesses. It does not run through the four winds.

On the synopsis, sackcloth begins when the worship of images is fully set up. He dates the 1,260 from 607 or from 800. Counted from 800, the term reaches 2060 and may end later. The note in Yahuda MS 7.3 says he wrote the figure to restrain people who announce the day and bring the prophecies into contempt.

In the Goth draft, sackcloth comes when the kingdom of the Ostrogoths fell and the kings of the Visigoths embraced the Roman Catholic religion. The two churches have already prophesied, without sackcloth, through the four first trumpets.

What was used

The drawings he left, and the sessions that read them

The synopsis on the diagram page is Yahuda MS 7.2a, the two holograph copies titled “A Synopsis of the Synchronisms of the Apocalypse.” The chart he sent Locke in 1691 is a smaller sheet, Bodleian MS Locke c. 27, f. 88r. This page is neither facsimile. The wind order, the twelve years after 395, and the emperor names follow the facing leaf and the essay on the seven heads. Where a later computation moves a date, the cell says so.

The two openings of the first four trumpets are the two propositions in THEM00214, already set side by side in the guide. The plague lengths are the ones he wrote in Yahuda MS 7.2. The 2060 note, and the bounds of the 1,290 and the 1,335, are Yahuda MS 7.3. The eight successive parts of the seventh seal, and the millennium as a day, are the Observations of 1733. The rule that the seals do not overlap, and that the trumpets, the thunders, and the vials are one set of seven wars, is the early Positions. The sentence that the killing of the witnesses had not happened is Yahuda MS 1.4.

The Visigoth and Ostrogoth reading is quoted from a local session that was asked for Newton’s description of the witnesses in 395, and for examples of the two witnesses using the power of their testimony. That session did not name a shelfmark. The wording is not in the synopsis reader, and this page does not write it into that reader.
